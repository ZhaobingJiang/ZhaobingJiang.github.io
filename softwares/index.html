2. FanAI Fan Optimization Agentlocal app

An end-to-end agent system for fan blade-profile optimization and efficiency upgrading in thermal power plants, built on a curated library of 29 optimization cases (60–1000 MW units: induced-draft / booster fans, forced-draft fans, primary-air fans, air-cooled fans, flue and air ducts, blades and guide vanes; every case carries plant and unit, equipment model, problem description, root cause, solution, expected effect, quality score and verification status, plus structured case parameters, solutions, effects and tags, and links to CFD cases / result files, blade profiles and 3-D models). A diagnosis combines case-based reasoning, GraphRAG retrieval (Neo4j knowledge graph + LanceDB vector store) and natural-language SQL over the case database, taking a plain-language problem statement and up to eight kinds of engineering documents (3-D fan model, duct layout, DCS operating data, performance curve, previous CFD report, equipment manual, site photos and blade parameters) as input. The reasoning stream is pushed live to the page; the structured diagnosis is then expanded into an HTML optimization-scheme report with automatically generated or matched technical figures and exported to Word. A Neo4j-based knowledge-graph editor supports graph browsing and editing with five layouts. Front end Vue 3 + Vite + Element Plus; business back end Spring Boot + MyBatis-Plus (25 REST controllers, 30 tables); agent service FastAPI + GraphRAG. All services run locally on the author's machine and data never leaves it.

29 optimization cases (24 verified) · 60–1000 MW units · 8 engineering-document types · CBR + GraphRAG diagnosis · HTML scheme report with figures + Word export · knowledge-graph editor · 25 REST controllers / 30 tables

3. Pollutant Dispersion Situation Forecasting Systemlocal app

A desktop forecasting system for atmospheric pollutant dispersion, driven by WRF 1 km meteorological fields and solved with both native SCIPUFF (second-order closure Gaussian puff) and a fast Gaussian CPU model. Concentration fields are rendered on satellite basemaps with a logarithmic scale, together with live cumulative covered area and affected population; source terms (release point by map picking or lon/lat, initial height and diameter), warning thresholds and the colour range are all configurable. Ships with 13 offline city cases — Beijing, Shanghai, Taipei, New York, Washington, London, Paris, Tokyo, Changsha, Dubai, Moscow, Berlin and Osaka — including wind fields, population rasters, administrative boundaries, buildings, tiles and pre-computed runs, so a full 2-hour, 61-frame simulation can be played back frame by frame without network access.

13 city cases · ~25 GB offline data · 2 h / 2 min → 61 frames · WRF 1 km · SCIPUFF + Gaussian CPU · 8-thread CPU solve · example: 185.65 km² covered / 210,203 people affected

4. 2-D Stokes Flow Deep-Learning Surrogatelocal app

An interactive surrogate model for Stokes flow past circles and ellipses in a channel. Trained on 2,400+ cases computed with FastBEM — the fast-multipole boundary-element solver for 2-D Stokes flow by Prof. Y. J. Liu (SUSTech) — the model predicts the full velocity field and the drag force on every obstacle in about 3 ms. The desktop edition runs as a local web interface and, beyond the millisecond surrogate predictions, adds FastBEM real-time cross-checking with per-obstacle error analysis and rolling re-training: every verified case is fed back to fine-tune the models in place.

112 × 28 CFD grid  ·  velocity-field median relative error 2.2%  ·  total-drag median relative error 2.6% (held-out test set)  ·  round-5 rolling training

5. LBM Karman Vortex Street Unsteady Surrogatelocal app

Interactive surrogate for the unsteady wake of a single cylinder at Re = 80–160. Training data: 50 lattice-Boltzmann (D2Q9) runs, each with 16 warm-up + 8 sampled shedding cycles (vorticity and streamwise-velocity frames plus lift/drag histories). Three models run side by side: a POD-harmonic dual branch (analytic time synthesis), an end-to-end physics-informed network (PINN), and a harmonic-enhanced PINN-v2 (Fourier features + harmonic coefficient fields + physics soft constraints). Slide Re and switch algorithms to compare synchronised vorticity / velocity animations, force histories and St / Cd / CLrms live.

321 × 161 lattice · Re 80–160 · vortex-street fidelity 0.90–0.95 (PINN-v2, held-out) · PyTorch (CPU)

6. Opatiya Numerical Wave Tank (CFD GUI)local app

A graphical front end for numerical-wave-tank CFD simulations, organising the complete workflow into seven form pages: tank geometry and water depth, 17 wave types with wave-generation and absorption options, floating bodies with 6-DOF rigid-body dynamics, three-directional mesh refinement and numerical wave gauges. The software builds the block-structured mesh automatically, writes the solver input, and runs MS-MPI multi-core parallel solving; while the solver runs, an embedded VTK render window loads every result frame and colours water and air by the sign of the level-set function in real time.

7. PIER-VIBE Pier Safety Assessment Systemlocal app

A physics-chain assessment suite for bridge-pier safety in cross-sea environments. Equilibrium scour depth follows HEC-18 with Melville-Coleman rate evolution; wave loads use the Morison equation; fatigue accumulates through B–E class S-N curves with Palmgren-Miner summation and Goodman mean-stress correction; scour development maps directly onto the pier's fundamental frequency, extracted by FFT from a reproducible 100 s acceleration history. The weighted BSHI index (scour 0.35 + fatigue 0.35 + drift 0.30) returns a four-level safety signal with operation guidance — every intermediate quantity visible, eleven physical parameters adjustable in real time.

5-level mechanism chain · 11 tunable parameters · four-level BSHI verdict · default case BSHI ≈ 0.88 (certified) · millisecond evaluation

8. Urban Point Cloud CFD Geometry Modeling Platformlocal web app

A browser-based demonstration platform that turns raw LiDAR point clouds and GIS building footprints into CFD-ready city geometry, following the six stages of the modelling workflow: multi-source data fusion, point-cloud pre-processing (statistical outlier removal plus CSF cloth-simulation classification), DEM terrain reconstruction, building extraction with footprint fitting and LoD1.2 / 1.3 / 2.2 extrusion, CFD-oriented simplification, and geometry cleaning with voxel volume-mesh generation. Five real city scenes ship with the platform (TU Delft campus, Delft suburbs, Cologne, Zurich old town and Eugene), each sampled to 120,000 class-stratified points; every number on screen is recomputed live in the browser or taken from measured offline pipeline records. Pure static site — HTML + CSS + native ES modules, zero third-party JS/CSS, no build step, zero external requests — with a self-written WebGL1 multi-layer 3D view and LaTeX-to-MathML formula rendering. All processing stays in the browser; no point cloud or mesh is ever uploaded.

6 stages · 5 real city scenes (120,000 points each) · 907 footprints / 722 buildings rebuilt / 99.0% success (Zurich) · 33 algorithms / 133 formulas / 34 technical figures · zero dependencies, no build step

9. Target-Tracking System Demolocal app

An interactive demo suite for a target-tracking (damage-environment modeling) system, four linked pages in one local web app. The tracking-simulation page renders a UAV model through three simulated sensors — coarse TV (640×512@50fps), fine TV (1920×1080) and mid-wave infrared (false colour) — with the full imaging degradation chain (atmosphere, optics/turbulence blur, pixel sampling, noise, quantisation, electronic de-rotation) switchable stage by stage, plus Johnson-criterion DRI probabilities and a search-to-lock tracking scenario. The sensor-modelling page solves the per-channel MTF chain, Nyquist sampling and Johnson detection/recognition/identification ranges (cross-checked against the modular Python code with zero deviation on 45/45 items). The damage-assessment page builds a UAV damage tree (20 component-failure events aggregated through AND/OR/voting gates) for laser-induced thermal damage, with Monte-Carlo probability curves, minimal cut sets and component importance. The strike-decision page ingests the damage results and engagement geometry (range, line-of-sight, target attitude, visibility) to grade damage difficulty, mark vulnerable components on the 3-D model, rank strike points with a 5-attribute AHP-TOPSIS model, and solve an energy/time-budgeted 0-1 strike plan, with probability-dwell curves, effect-prediction surface and CSV/Markdown/JSON/PNG export.

four linked pages  ·  7-stage switchable degradation chain  ·  damage tree with 20 basic events, Monte-Carlo N=2000  ·  AHP-TOPSIS strike planning  ·  pure front-end, zero external dependencies

10. Zuiti — Voice Comeback Assistant for Immunization Clinicslocal app

A voice assistant for the vaccination window of a community hospital. The doctor clicks once; the app listens to the patient through the computer microphone and, about 1.5 s after the patient stops, answers out loud in a clipped northeastern-Chinese register that goads rather than argues: name the demand as out of line, hand the consequence straight back, send them off. Three intensity levels (mild / full / park marshal), 14 built-in confrontations with zero-latency canned lines and pre-generated audio, five voices and three speech rates (default: northeastern female at +35%). Speech recognition runs locally on CPU with faster-whisper, so the patient's words never leave the machine; replies stream from DeepSeek and are synthesized sentence by sentence. The interface deliberately shows no transcript at all — status light, latency read-out and voice only.

14 scenarios · 0 ms canned reply · first token 0.5–0.8 s · local ASR 1.4–1.6 s · ≈1.5–2.1 s from silence to spoken retort · faster-whisper small (int8, CPU) · edge-tts at +35% · voice-only UI
