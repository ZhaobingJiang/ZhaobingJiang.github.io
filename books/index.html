Books

Beyond journal papers and engineering practice, Zhaobing Jiang writes long-form books for general readers: some turn the economics and technology shifts of our time into judgements and actions ordinary people can use; others work history, legend and the present through fiction. All twenty-one complete works below are published in full and free to read or download, including the complete novels The Ledger (Zhangben), Genggeng (Unforgotten), In Search of Patrons (Xunzhao Dalao) and Well Water Mirrors (Jingshui Zhaoren). The two engineering monographs come first, and the rest follow in reverse order of completion. The fiction includes the zhanghui historical legend Su Dan Riding a White Crane; the modern long-form legend Apricot Blossoms Deep Behind Moling Pass; the sci-fi historical novel Weiyang; the novel Renxing; the rural novel Ouyechong; the adapted novel Rose Manor, after Hai Cheng; the historical novel Lipin Jinling, drawn from Yu Huai’s Banqiao Zaji; the campus novel Taoli Zhixia; the suspense novel Crop Circle; the novel Genggeng (Unforgotten); the coming-of-age novel In Search of Patrons (Xunzhao Dalao); and the documentary novel The Rise and Fall of an Enterprise (Qiye de Huihuang yu Moluo), on family succession, debt and the limits of a founder’s grip.

02 Engineering monograph

Dynamic Analysis of Fluid–Structure Interaction in Floating Bodies and Floating Multi-Body Systems

Qing Shen, Xujun Chen, Zhaobing Jiang · 2019 manuscript · Ten chapters

Written jointly by Qing Shen, Xujun Chen and Zhaobing Jiang: Shen wrote Chapters 1, 2, 3, 4, 7 and 8, Chen wrote Chapter 5, and Jiang wrote Chapters 6, 9 and 10, with Shen preparing the final manuscript. Starting from simplified and linearized models of different floating bodies and floating multi-body systems, the text develops the corresponding theoretical frameworks and solution schemes, and gives a systematic treatment of the underlying theory, numerical techniques and dynamic response characteristics — rigid-body dynamics, velocity-potential problems in fluid–structure interaction, hydrodynamic coefficients, and time-domain analysis of floating and floating-base multi-body systems. It is intended as a reference for graduate students and engineers working in naval architecture and ocean engineering, port, coastal and offshore engineering, combat engineering support and engineering mechanics.

The manuscript is published here in two parts, Chapters 1–7 (185 pages, including preface and contents) and Chapters 8–10 (73 pages), each available as PDF and Word. In the Word files the illustrations were re-compressed to display resolution; the text, equations and layout are unchanged.

03 Historical novel (zhanghui)

Su Dan Riding a White Crane: A Zhanghui-Form Historical Legend

Zhaobing Jiang · Zhanghui-form historical legend · 2026 · Five volumes and forty chapters · About 270,000 Chinese characters · Complete online
Chinese title: 《苏耽跨鹤》

Web edition · read the complete book online

A historical legend written in the Ming–Qing zhanghui manner. It tells of Su Dan, a boy of Guiyang Commandery in the early Western Han who was said to have been conceived from a five-coloured water plant, abandoned in a mountain cave, suckled by a white deer and sheltered by a white crane, to have cured a plague with well water and orange leaves, and at thirteen to have ridden a crane into the sky from Mount Niupi — the hill later renamed Suxian Ling (Suxian's Ridge) after him, still the city's landmark. In front of Chenzhou's railway station stands a statue of Su Dan riding the crane; the image has become the city's emblem and spiritual totem. The book tells the story of the people behind the totem.

The legend makes the son a god; the book keeps its centre on the mother who would not explain herself — on the shame, the silence and the daily labour that raised him, and on what it costs a woman to send her son away. Around that centre it follows the two thousand years in which the legend was rewritten — Tang stele, Song honours, the Three Wonders Stele, a Ming travelogue, Qing scepticism, and the present-day heritage site — so that the question finally turns back on the legend itself: what is remembered, and by whom.

Behind the legend, there is a mother.

04 Popular science

The Most Beautiful Mathematical Figures in History: Where Science Meets Art

Zhaobing Jiang · 2026 · 228 pages · About 100,000 Chinese characters · Seven parts and fifty chapters · 148 illustrations
Chinese title: 《史上最美数学图形：科学与艺术的结合》

PDF (full text) DOCX download Web edition · all 148 figures, read online

This book is an illustrated tour of fifty classical mathematical figures, from the Fibonacci spiral, the golden rectangle and the cardioid to the Mandelbrot set, the Lorenz attractor, the Möbius strip, the Klein bottle, Penrose tilings and the Ulam spiral. It opens with a prologue on why mathematics is beautiful and proceeds through seven parts — golden ratios and spirals, the beauty of curves, the world of fractals, the beauty of chaos, the beauty of space, symmetry and tessellation, and patterns born from arithmetic — closing with an epilogue that treats mathematics and art as two languages of one beauty.

Each chapter starts from a phenomenon in nature, art or everyday life, explains the mathematics behind it, and plots the figure at high resolution: rabbit breeding and galaxy arms, nautilus shells and sunflower seeds at 137.5 degrees, the three-body problem, Turing patterns, the seventeen wallpaper groups, prime spirals and the digits of π. Appendices gather further reading, image credits and an index of the plotting code for every chapter, so that readers can reproduce each figure themselves.

05 Popular science

The Engineering of Luck: Foundations of a Discipline of Fortune

Zhaobing Jiang · 2026 · 264 pages · About 170,000 Chinese characters · Four parts and seventeen chapters · Appendices A–G
Chinese title: 《运气工程学》

PDF (full text) DOCX download Web edition · read the whole book online

The companion volume to Survival Economics turns in the opposite direction: instead of avoiding formulas, it sets out to establish luck as a research object in its own right — postulates, terminology, a taxonomy of five mechanisms across three scales, models, measurement schemes, intervention tools and falsifiable acceptance propositions. A five-stage process loop (exposure, recognition, conversion, amplification, convergence) links the argument together, and the same logic is carried from the individual to the team and the platform. Appendices A–G give a glossary and symbol table, a tool kit, reproducible worked examples, a map of the academic conversation, a luck audit form, a case library and a quick-reference table of the twelve tradition-mapped factors. 264 pages: preface, seventeen chapters in four parts, seven appendices.

06 Popular science

Survival Economics in Uncertain Times: A Guide to Observing and Acting in a Changing World

Zhaobing Jiang · Third edition (expanded manuscript) · 2026 · 132 pages · About 131,000 Chinese characters · Three parts and fifteen chapters · Appendices A–C
Chinese title: 《不确定年代的生存经济学——写给普通人的变局观察与行动指南》

PDF (full text) DOCX download Web edition · read the whole book online

This book is written for ordinary people who feel the ground shifting under their feet. In the past few years we have witnessed events that read like textbook cases: global inflation returning, a bank collapsing within 48 hours, house prices ending their one-way rise, AI rewriting the prospects of countless occupations within months, and delayed retirement moving from rumour to timetable. Behind every headline are concrete questions for ordinary households: where should savings go when deposit rates keep falling? Should a shrinking home be paid off early? What if a child graduates into a market where three people compete for one post? Will a 35-year-old appear on the next layoff list?

The answers available today tend to sit at two extremes: academic narratives that are rigorous but far from a household ledger, and traffic-driven anxiety that is intense but offers no defensible remedy. This book stands on the ground between them: it uses Nobel-level economic theory to make sense of the shift that is under way, then translates that theory into actions an ordinary person can realistically take.

The book has three parts, each with five chapters of five sections. Part I, "Shifting Ground", answers what is happening: inflation and money, the mountain of debt, the retreat of globalization, the AI revolution, and ageing with low growth — five main lines that form the coordinate system of our time. Part II, "Lenses", answers why: cycles, human nature, information, institutions, and risk — five pairs of theory glasses tested by Nobel Prizes, which reveal the mechanism behind the phenomenon. Part III, "Playbook", answers what to do: a household balance-sheet checkup, career and human-capital strategy, investment discipline, survival rules for small business owners, and stress testing — five sets of tools that can be put to work directly. Every section follows the same logic: story, mechanism, domestic relevance, theory, action.

Three things the book does not do: it makes no point-in-time forecasts, it uses no mathematical formulas (all theory is explained through stories, analogies and charts), and it promises no shortcut to sudden wealth. Its tone is stability before return, survival before prosperity, discipline before cleverness. The domestic livelihood data it cites — graduate numbers, unemployment, foreclosed homes, deposit rates, the delayed-retirement timetable — come from public reports and official statistics as of mid-2026. Data ages; the mechanisms behind it do not.

07 Historical legend

Apricot Blossoms Deep Behind Moling Pass: A Historical Legend

Zhaobing Jiang · Historical legend · 2026 · Prologue, four volumes and sixty-seven chapters, plus two epilogues · About 260,000 Chinese characters · Complete online
Chinese title: 《杏花深处秣陵关》

Web edition · read the complete book online

A long historical legend built around Moling, the ancient name of Nanjing. In the Jian'an years of the late Han, Sun Quan moved his seat to Moling, raised the Stone City and renamed Moling as Jianye; in those years of change, eight ordinary people — a failed fangshi, a lame coroner, a butcher, an old shamaness, a dyer's daughter, a beggar boy, a deserter turned musician, and a county clerk — were hired to "make" a god out of a dead petty county commandant. They staged omens, drew maps, hid a bone and dodged investigations, turning a drunken boast ("my bones are clean; dead, I shall become a god") step by step into truth. And the god they made, in turn, made something of those eight people.

From the old Qin prophecy that "a ruler's aura will rise in Jiangdong five hundred years hence" to the apparitions of Jiang Ziwen, the Lady of the Blue Stream and the white horse crossing the river, every layer of the legend lands back among market alleys and working people: a coroner reading bones, a stonecutter keeping night watch, a gatekeeper who would not leave his pass, a temple servant carving names into bricks, storytellers passing a city's memory down the generations. The complete book — a prologue, four volumes of sixty-seven chapters and two epilogues, some 260,000 Chinese characters — is published here in full.

The city has a new name. What lies beneath it has not changed.

08 Sci-fi historical novel

Weiyang (Unending): A Sci-Fi Historical Novel

Zhaobing Jiang · Sci-fi historical novel · 2026 · Five volumes and seventy chapters · About 260,000 Chinese characters · Complete online
Chinese title: 《未央》 · Subtitle: 历史与未来并存，长生共孤独同在 (History and the future coexist; longevity and solitude are the same solitude)

Web edition · read the complete book online

Emperor Wu of Han, who had sought immortality for fifty years, dies alone in the Wuzuo Palace with a single lamp beside him. He wakes in a white room, in a forty-year-old body, hearing a water clock, and is told: this is Yunjing, the carbon–silicon symbiosis of 2050 — you can live forever. Placed back into a Han dynasty rebuilt from his own memories, he reigns again without knowing it, until he finds a white slip in an old storehouse that reads "Subject 73".

Seventy-three iterations, seventy-two earlier selves, a world-maker named Taiyi, a woman who spent sixty years restoring his archive, and a lamp that refuses to go out from Chang'an to Yunjing. The historical line is written half in the prose of the Shiji and the Hanshu; the future line cuts in cold and plain, and between the chapters run the "Taiyi logs", which begin to pull the ground out from under the story from the middle of Volume Two onward.

History and the future coexist; longevity and solitude are the same solitude.

09 Full-length novel

Renxing (Human Form): A Novel

Zhaobing Jiang · Full-length novel · 2026 · Prologue, four volumes and sixty-five chapters, plus three epilogues · About 300,000 Chinese characters · Complete online
Chinese title: 《人形》 · Subtitle: 造人者，何以为人 (Those who make human-form robots — what keeps them human?)

Web edition · read the complete book online

Gu Changfeng, an overseas-educated doctor, builds a humanoid-robot company: listed in ten years, busted overnight, and vanished one night — no one knows where. His college classmate Chen Shouzhuo, a homegrown engineer who never studied abroad, keeps a numerical-simulation company alive for ten years computing aero-engines — and the wind-tunnel test matches his numbers on the first try. Two roads, two men, one city — and between them the neon of nightclubs, the teacups of officials, the signatures of academicians, workers demanding back pay, and a girl who memorized a lie a hundred times.

A prologue, four volumes of sixty-five chapters and three epilogues: Making Men, Human Form, Deformation, Returning to Form. It is about the man who makes human-form robots, and about the "form" every person in an era chooses for themselves. One system of imagery runs through the whole book — water, lamps, the wind tunnel, and the number forty-seven.

The wind lifts people into the sky; the wind tunnel holds them steady on the ground.

10 Rural novel

Ouyechong: A Village Chronicle

Zhaobing Jiang · Rural novel · 2026 · Prologue, four volumes and sixty-four chapters · About 240,000 Chinese characters · Complete online
Chinese title: 《欧冶冲》 · Subtitle: 水不认得名字，名字认得水 (Water knows no names; but names, water remembers.)

Web edition · read the complete book online

Ouyechong is a small Jiang-surname hamlet in Shuanghe Village, Yangtang Township, southern Hunan, pressed between four larger clans surnamed Chen, Li, Wang and Liu. Across a hundred years and four generations the Jiangs live by seven ways out — study, soldiering, marriage, sworn kinship, dependence, endurance, leaving — through land reform, collectivization, steel smelting, famine, canal digging, the Cultural Revolution, the division of fields and water, feuds, migrant labour, village mergers, hollowing out, and at last the compiling of the clan genealogy.

Written as a historian's chronicle: the volumes are annals, the land and customs are treatises, the people are biographies, and each volume closes with a list of doubts. It is a book about water, land and houses, and about the secret ledger hidden behind every gift book — and about how names are finally written back.

Water knows no names; but names, water remembers.

In the spring of 1999 a reporter on the provincial paper, Yang Tianchen, receives an unsigned letter: his classmate Meng Ning has died in Luhai, and the police call it a fall from a hotel window. Yang goes to Luhai.

There is the Aladdin Hotel with its eighteenth-floor nightclub, a farm boy turned tycoon named Shen Huachang, and the Rose Manor: three buildings, one rule each — no phones in Building One, no names in Building Two, no prying in Building Three. A rose in the hills is a flower; a rose on the table is a price.

12 Historical novel

Lipin Jinling (Beauties of Jinling): A Historical Novel

Zhaobing Jiang · Historical novel · 2026 · Prologue, four volumes and thirty-two chapters, plus an epilogue · About 235,000 Chinese characters · Complete online
Chinese title: 《丽品金陵》 · Subtitle: 秦淮旧院 · 十二钗传 · Based on Yu Huai's Banqiao Zaji (c. 1690s), whose full text is appended and 66 passages from it are woven into the narrative

Web edition · read the complete book online

In the last years of the Ming the lantern boats on the Qinhuai never went dark. Twelve women left their names in the old quarter: Li Shiniang, Gu Mei, Dong Bai, Bian Sai, Ge Nen, Wang Yue, Kou Mei, Li Daniang, Dun Wen, Li Mei, Ma Jiao and Fan Jue. They wrote verses on tavern walls, painted orchids for a jug of wine, sang roles on the stage, and chose — some of them — how they would die.

Then came 1644 and the fall of Nanjing. The lantern boats scattered, the painted towers were sealed: one married a merchant, one became a nun, one followed her husband into Fujian and died on the city wall, one ended up teaching songs to village girls by a market bridge. Thirty years later, in Suzhou, an old scholar who once wrote their verses, their manifestos and their accounts sits down under a lamp and writes their names back — this is Yu Huai's Banqiao Zaji, and this novel grows out of it.

Four volumes — Lantern Boats, the Painted Tower, Fire and War, White Hair — interweave two timelines: 1690s Suzhou, where Yu Huai writes; and 1638–1670s, where the women and their scholars live and die. The prologue opens the box, the epilogue closes the album, and the full text of Banqiao Zaji is appended for comparison.

Lanterns lit the night; the war took the rest.

13 Campus novel

Taoli Zhixia (Beneath the Peach and Plum): A Campus Novel

Zhaobing Jiang · Campus novel · 2026 · Twenty-eight chapters · About 275,000 Chinese characters · Complete online
Chinese title: 《桃李之下》 · A law school in Jiangzhou, 2021–2024

Web edition · read the complete book online

In the autumn of 2021 Chen Mo enters the law school of Jiangzhou University as a master's student and joins the seminar of Shen Wangshu: vice dean, editor of the Jiangzhou Law Review, and a scholar with a great many friends.

A second-author line on a paper, a 120,000-yuan page fee, a transcript that has been edited: in the pocket of his coat he presses record for the first time. Over three years he watches how places, signatures, projects and silence are traded inside the door — until the letter he posts comes back to him as procedure: a notice, a talk, a mediation, a hearing, a notice again.

Told in three first-person voices — Chen Mo, Su Wan, Zhou Heng — it is not a story about one person, and not about one university.

The peach and plum do not speak; yet a path wears itself beneath them. And those on the path walk away.

14 Suspense novel

Crop Circle (Maitian Guiquan): A Suspense Novel

Zhaobing Jiang · Suspense novel · 2026 · Three volumes, forty chapters · About 245,000 Chinese characters · Complete online
Chinese title: 《麦田怪圈》 · A wheat village in northern Anhui, 2012 and 2024

Web edition · read the complete book online

In 2012 a young reporter, Lin Shen, covered a crop-circle hoax in a wheat village and won a prize. That same summer a fifteen-year-old girl, Jiang Xiaosui, disappeared.

Twelve years later her father sows a sixty-metre human face into the same field with two varieties of wheat, seven days apart. A drone films it; the internet calls it a miracle; visitors, flour sellers, consultants and a two-hundred-million-yuan "Phoenix Project" arrive. Lin Shen sees at once that the wheat has not been flattened but sown — and that the old man wants only for one reporter to look again at an old aerial photograph, where a figure in a red vest stands just outside the hoax.

Three volumes, forty chapters, two timelines. The circle is fake; the money is real; so is the body. No one confesses, because the killing was a collective decision.

The wheat will remember. People will not.

15 Novel · complete

The Ledger (Zhangben): A Novel

Zhaobing Jiang · Novel · 1953–2023 · Tongwei, Gansu · Five volumes, forty-six chapters · About 310,000 Chinese characters · Complete (46/46 chapters)
Chinese title: 《账本》 · Subtitle: 一个饥饿者的七十年 (One Hungry Man’s Seventy Years)

Web edition · read the full text

In February 1953 a boy is born in Zhangjiachà, a village in Tongwei, Gansu. His father opens a cloth-bound ledger, names him Mancang — “Barn-Full” — and adds three characters: grain undetermined.

In 1960 his parents die in the famine. His mother pushes the last half potato into his mouth; his elder sister is given away for two dou of millet. For the next sixty years he keeps every gain and loss in his head, calculating his way out of the loess, and calculating his family, his wife, his lovers and his students into entries in a ledger.

In 2020 the auditors arrive. He discovers that the one debt he has recorded all his life cannot be balanced.

Compassion without absolution.

16 Classical Chinese · annotated edition

Yulizi (The Master of Yuli): An Annotated Edition

Liu Ji (original) · Jiang Zhaobing (annotations) · Classical Chinese · Ming dynasty · Eighteen chapters, 180 pieces and 200 paragraphs · Complete translation and 1,123 notes · Complete online
Chinese title: Yulizi Zhushu (鬱離子注疏) · Original: Liu Ji (Liu Bowen, 1311–1375) · Base text: Sibu Congkan facsimile of the 1572 He Tang edition

Web edition · read the complete annotated book online

Yulizi is the collection of fables and political meditations Liu Ji — Liu Bowen, the Ming dynasty strategist — wrote while in retirement at the end of the Yuan. Its 180 pieces, gathered in eighteen chapters, pass judgement on the men and institutions of a collapsing order: a horse judged by its birthplace rather than its gait, a lute-maker who must forge antiquity to be believed, a beekeeper whose heirs let the hive die, a physician who prescribes poison.

This edition is built for learners rather than specialists. The base text is the Sibu Congkan facsimile of the 1572 He Tang edition, given in the original traditional characters; a simplified transcription, a complete modern Chinese translation and 1,123 notes — definitions, sources, paraphrase and textual collation — accompany every paragraph, with rare characters glossed for pronunciation.

A Ming minister's book of fables, read with the sources open beside it.

17 Novel · complete

Genggeng (“Unforgotten”): A Novel

Zhaobing Jiang · Novel · 1976–2031 · Fengshuchong, central Hunan · Five volumes, forty-nine chapters · About 300,000 Chinese characters · Complete (49/49 chapters)
Chinese title: 《耿耿》 · Subtitle: 一部关于克制与欲望的乡土长篇小说 (A Local Novel of Restraint and Desire)

Web edition · read the full text

In the sixth lunar month of 1976 a boy is born in Fengshuchong, a village in central Hunan. His grandmother carries him to the old camphor tree at the mouth of the valley and gives him to the tree as a godchild. At the tree she says only: “Hesheng, do not be afraid.”

He grows up praised for being sensible. Being sensible means swallowing what he wants to say and pulling back the hand he wants to extend. At sixteen, in a camphor grove in the county town, he meets a girl who paints. She looks at him and he looks away; she writes, and he writes back. The letters grow shorter; the road grows longer.

Military academy, sentry duty in northern snow, a marriage, a flat, debts, a daughter, the epidemic, ageing parents. One year in middle age he walks into a bathhouse and walks out again; years later he goes in. At home he writes the money in his ledger, lightly, as if it were someone else's business.

Desire is translated into letters, into marks on examination papers, into years of silence. Her restraint is a choice; his is fear.

A village boy learns to hold back — and becomes, in that holding back, a whole man.

18 Novel · complete

In Search of Patrons (Xunzhao Dalao): A Novel

Zhaobing Jiang · Novel · 1989–2026 · Chenjia’ao · Hangzhou · Boston · Prologue and five volumes, forty chapters · About 360,000 Chinese characters · Complete (40/40 chapters)
Chinese title: 《寻找大佬》 · Subtitle: 一个寒门子弟的三十七年 (A Poor Boy’s Thirty-Seven Years)

Web edition · read the full text

On the fifteenth of the first lunar month, 1989, seven-year-old Chen Zhiqiang rides his father’s shoulders to watch the village shehuo parade. After the crowd disperses, his father is slapped by a richer villager over the irrigation water. The boy hides behind the door and says nothing.

That slap stays with him for thirty-seven years. A village teacher gives him a torn Xinhua Dictionary, missing the page for lao — the word for a big man, someone who can get things done. A village secretary writes six characters on the flyleaf of a notebook: remember kindness, not names. A professor gives him a key to a lab; a general sets a family dinner; an advisor at Stanford tells him he is not short of intelligence but of a problem. One by one he finds the people who can pull him up — and one by one he turns them into entries.

County exam, engineering school, a state research institute, Purdue, Stanford, MIT, a start-up in Hangzhou. Every step upward changes the face of his inferiority: the computer he cannot switch on, the toast he cannot make, the accent they laugh at, the paper rejected seven times, the investor who asks “why you?”

In September 2026, in a lecture hall at MIT, a doctoral student asks him: “How did you find your patrons?” Standing on the stage, he sees the boy behind the door in 1989.

He was not looking for a patron. He was looking for someone to hit back for his father.

19 Novel · complete

Well Water Mirrors (Jingshui Zhaoren): A Novel

Zhaobing Jiang · Novel · 1986–2026 · Lingshui · Yunzhou · Huaidong · Prologue and three volumes, forty chapters · About 250,000 Chinese characters · Complete (40/40 chapters)
Chinese title: 《井水照人》 · Subtitle: 井水照人，照见的是各人的心 (Well water mirrors the heart of each who looks into it)

Web edition · read the full text

In the summer of 1993 the village of Chengjiawei has gone forty days without rain. Seven-year-old Cheng Jianshan falls into the old well at the head of the village. His mother lowers the rope; he cannot hold on. A peddler hooks him out with a carrying pole.

She slaps him, then holds him and says: well water mirrors the heart of each who looks into it. He remembers that sentence for the next thirty-three years. First place in the township exam, third in the county, 683 out of 750 in the 2004 college entrance exam. He tears the score sheet up and throws it into the stove; his mother pulls it out of the fire, pieces it together, wraps it in plastic and hides it in a camphor chest for twenty-two years.

He marries a girl from the city; thirty tables at the wedding, two of them his. On their wedding night he cannot. Her name alone is on the deed. She has an ectopic pregnancy and surgery while he is on exercise; he learns last. Divorced, he leaves with one box of books. He marries again, has twins, leaves the army, and starts a company that builds five-axis CNC machine tools. In the winter of 2024 the first machine passes its test cut, and the retired chief engineer sits down at the workshop door and cries.

In the spring of 2026 his mother dies of a cerebral haemorrhage. He goes back to Chengjiawei and opens the camphor chest: the scorched score sheet, the line his sister wrote for his mother — keep it safe — and, at the bottom, a crooked character his mother practised for half a year.

He spent a lifetime proving himself, only to find that some things never needed proving.

20 Documentary investigation · complete

The Rise and Fall of an Enterprise (Qiye de Huihuang yu Moluo): Ten Chinese Family Firms and the Succession That Failed

Zhaobing Jiang · Documentary · China · 1987–2026 · Ten cases + prologue, comparison and two appendices · About 212,000 Chinese characters · Complete
Chinese title: 《企业的辉煌与没落》 · Subtitle: 十家企业，十种死法，一个病：接不住 · Appendices: 真实案例对照表, 资料来源与证据分级

Web edition · read the full text

Ten Chinese private companies. Ten successions. One disease: no one able to take over.

Haixin Steel, Lifan, Fuguiniao, Chuying, Shuanghui, Wahaha, Metersbonwe, Shanshan, Yurun and Suning — steel, motorcycles, shoes, pigs, pork, soft drinks, clothes, materials, food and retail. In every case the founder was shot, died, was taken away, grew old, or ran out of lenders before the question of who comes next had an answer.

Each chapter is reconstructed from court rulings, listed-company announcements, annual reports and published reporting. Real names, real dates, real numbers. No invented dialogue, no invented interiors, no invented motives; direct quotations come from published sources and are attributed.
